## What SOLIDWORKS Brings to 3D Design Workflows

SOLIDWORKS provides a comprehensive environment for developing detailed three-dimensional mechanical designs. Its workflow combines parametric modeling, assemblies, technical drawings, simulation, and documentation within a structured engineering workspace.

Parametric modeling allows design elements to remain connected to defined dimensions and relationships. This makes it possible to modify a component while maintaining the intended structure of the surrounding geometry.

Part modeling provides tools for developing individual mechanical components. Features, sketches, dimensions, and geometric relationships can be organized into a structured model that can be refined throughout the design process.

Assembly design extends the workflow from individual components to complete mechanical systems. Multiple parts can be combined and positioned according to their intended relationships, allowing users to evaluate how components interact.

Technical drawings provide a way to communicate manufacturing and engineering information from the three-dimensional model. Dimensions, annotations, views, and drawing details can be organized into structured documentation.

SOLIDWORKS also supports simulation-oriented workflows. Engineering teams can evaluate design behavior and investigate how models may respond under defined conditions before moving toward physical production.

Finite element analysis capabilities can assist with evaluating structural behavior. These workflows can provide additional insight into a design and help identify areas that may require further engineering attention.

Flow Simulation extends analysis toward fluid and thermal behavior. Such capabilities can be useful for engineering projects where airflow, fluid movement, temperature, or related physical effects are important.

SOLIDWORKS CAM connects design data with manufacturing-oriented workflows. This can help bridge the gap between creating a model and preparing appropriate machining operations for supported production processes.

3D printing workflows can also benefit from a structured CAD environment. A detailed model can serve as the basis for preparing physical prototypes, design verification, or additive manufacturing projects.

Rendering capabilities provide tools for creating visual representations of finished designs. These visualizations can be useful for presentations, design reviews, product communication, and concept evaluation.

eDrawings provides a complementary way to review and communicate supported CAD designs. It can be useful when project participants need to inspect design information without working directly inside the primary modeling environment.

Product data management capabilities help organize engineering information across larger projects. Structured file and revision workflows can make it easier to maintain consistency when multiple design resources are involved.

3DEXPERIENCE-related workflows extend the broader SOLIDWORKS environment toward connected design and collaboration. These capabilities can support teams that need to manage design information across multiple stages of product development.

For mechanical engineers and product designers, SOLIDWORKS brings modeling, assemblies, drawings, analysis, and manufacturing preparation into a unified workflow. This reduces the need to treat each design stage as a completely separate process.

The software is also suitable for education and structured learning. Students can use the modeling environment to develop practical understanding of mechanical design principles, parametric relationships, assemblies, and technical documentation.

SOLIDWORKS therefore provides a complete environment for turning engineering concepts into structured digital models. Its combination of 3D CAD, mechanical design, simulation, documentation, and manufacturing-oriented tools supports a broad product-development workflow.

## Starting a SOLIDWORKS 3D Design Session

**Prerequisites:** Prepare the design requirements, reference geometry, dimensions, and supporting engineering information needed for the project.

1. Launch SOLIDWORKS from the Windows desktop environment.
2. Create a new part, assembly, or drawing document according to the project requirements.
3. Build the initial sketches and establish the required dimensions and geometric relationships.
4. Develop the 3D model and add components when working on an assembly.
5. Review the design using drawings, simulation, rendering, or other appropriate engineering tools.
6. Save the completed project and prepare the resulting design data for documentation, review, manufacturing, or further development.
